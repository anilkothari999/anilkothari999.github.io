Mutual Funds

Mutual funds pool investors’ money and invest according to a scheme mandate. Suitability depends on your goals, time horizon and ability to tolerate losses.

Understand before you invest

How it works

Equity funds invest primarily in shares and can be volatile; debt funds invest in fixed-income instruments and carry interest-rate and credit risks; hybrid funds combine asset classes; ELSS funds offer tax benefits subject to prevailing tax rules and lock-in; index funds aim to track an index, subject to tracking error.

Potential benefits

Mutual funds may provide diversified exposure through professionally managed portfolios, but values can fall as well as rise.

Who may consider it?

Equity: longer horizons and higher risk capacity. Debt: goal-specific fixed-income exposure after credit and duration review. Hybrid: mixed allocation. ELSS: investors who understand the lock-in and tax rules. Index: investors seeking index-linked exposure.

Important risk note: Fund returns are not guaranteed. Read scheme information documents, key information memoranda and riskometers before investing.

Specialised Investment Fund (SIF)

SIFs are a regulated investment-fund category with specified strategy and investor eligibility requirements. They are not a substitute for understanding the underlying strategy.

Understand before you invest

How it works

Eligibility and minimum investment rules depend on current SEBI regulations and the specific offering. Verify the latest offer documents, distributor availability, redemption terms and strategy risks before proceeding.

Potential benefits

Potential access to differentiated strategies and portfolio construction, with strategy-specific risk controls and disclosures.

Who may consider it?

Investors who meet the applicable eligibility and minimum investment requirements and can understand more complex strategies and higher risks.

Important risk note: SIF strategies may use derivatives, leverage or concentrated positions. Capital loss is possible. Confirm current minimums and eligibility from the official offer documents; do not rely on this page as a live regulatory update.

Portfolio Management Service (PMS)

PMS provides portfolio management under an agreement with a registered portfolio manager. Portfolio holdings and terms depend on the service selected.

Understand before you invest

How it works

Review the portfolio manager’s registration, strategy, fees, benchmark, custody, reporting and exit terms. Performance may differ materially from benchmarks and from other clients’ portfolios.

Potential benefits

Portfolio customisation, professional management and reporting, subject to the agreement and applicable regulations.

Who may consider it?

Eligible investors who meet the prevailing minimum-ticket requirements and can tolerate portfolio concentration, market volatility and fees.

Important risk note: PMS is market-linked and can involve concentration and liquidity risks. Verify the current regulatory minimum and all charges with the registered portfolio manager.

Home / Fixed Deposits

Fixed Deposits

Fixed deposits allow money to be placed with a bank or other eligible issuer for a defined tenure and stated interest terms.

Understand before you invest

How it works

Compare effective yield, compounding, premature withdrawal rules, tax treatment, deposit insurance eligibility and issuer creditworthiness. Corporate deposits generally have different risks from bank deposits.

Potential benefits

Predictable contractual interest terms, choice of tenure and options for interest payout, subject to issuer terms.

Who may consider it?

People seeking a defined-tenure deposit who understand liquidity restrictions, taxation and issuer risk.

Important risk note: Interest rates and terms vary. Corporate deposits can carry credit risk. Deposit insurance is subject to statutory limits and eligibility; do not assume every deposit is fully insured.

Home / RBI & Corporate Bonds

RBI & Corporate Bonds

Bonds are debt instruments. Returns depend on the instrument terms, issuer payments and the price paid when buying or selling.

Understand before you invest

How it works

Understand coupon, yield-to-maturity, maturity date, credit rating, seniority, liquidity and tax treatment. A bond’s market price may fall when interest rates rise.

Potential benefits

Potential interest income and portfolio diversification, depending on issuer, maturity and structure.

Who may consider it?

Investors who understand fixed-income products and can evaluate credit, interest-rate and liquidity risks.

Important risk note: Bond investments are not risk-free. Ratings are opinions, not guarantees. Corporate issuers may default; secondary-market prices can fluctuate.

Home / 54EC Capital Gains Bonds

54EC Capital Gains Bonds

Section 54EC of the Income-tax Act may allow eligible taxpayers to claim exemption for qualifying long-term capital gains invested in specified bonds, subject to statutory conditions.

Understand before you invest

How it works

Eligibility, investment deadline, eligible issuers, investment limits and lock-in are defined by the law and bond issue terms. Obtain tax advice for your facts and verify the current rules before investing.

Potential benefits

May support tax planning when a qualifying capital gain and eligible investment meet the current law.

Who may consider it?

Taxpayers with eligible long-term capital gains from specified assets who can meet statutory timelines and lock-in requirements.

Important risk note: Tax rules can change and eligibility is fact-specific. This page is informational, not tax advice. Verify the current statute and issue documents.
